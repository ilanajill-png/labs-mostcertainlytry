The harvest ledger

Every batch should answer the useful questions.

The public De Kiewiet Associates biography documents decades of systems and food-processing experience. It does not document a beekeeping history. This project begins with the honest part: build the evidence record before building the legend.

  1. 01

    Who tended the bees?

    Name the beekeeper or apiary partner, their region, and what husbandry facts can actually be supported.

  2. 02

    Where did the nectar come from?

    Define “local,” record the forage landscape, and avoid monofloral claims without appropriate evidence.

  3. 03

    What happened after harvest?

    Publish extraction date, heating/filtration statement, ingredients, net weight, and lot code.

  4. 04

    What does this batch taste like?

    Write notes from the jar in hand and explain crystallization as a natural change, not a defect.

Our approach

Measure what matters. Claim only what can be shown.

01

Bee-first evidence

Turn good intentions into disclosed practices. Avoid welfare or pesticide claims until the apiary can document them.

02

Ingredient clarity

Every infusion lists every ingredient. No proprietary “wellness blend” fog.

03

Useful packaging

Glass where feasible, readable labels, lot coding, reuse guidance, and no decorative packaging that overwhelms the jar.

04

No medical theater

Honey is food. Botanical jars are sold for flavor and kitchen use, without disease or allergy claims.

FAQ

Before ordering opens.

Can I buy these jars now?

Not yet. This is a sales-ready concept page, not a live inventory claim. Ordering should open only after source, labeling, food-law pathway, costs, and fulfillment are confirmed.

Is the honey raw or local?

No such claim is being made yet. The finished page should define processing and geography using the verified batch record.

Why might honey crystallize?

Crystallization is a normal physical change influenced by floral composition, temperature, and time. Final care instructions should match the actual product.

Is honey safe for infants?

No. Honey should not be fed to children under 12 months.

Are botanical honeys medicine?

No. The concept presents infused honey as food for culinary enjoyment and makes no disease-treatment or prevention claim.
