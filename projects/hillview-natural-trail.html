Hiking field guide · San Antonio

Hillview Natural Trail

A perimeter circuit through dry creek country, oak-juniper woodland, limestone slopes, and elevated Hill Country habitat at Dwight D. Eisenhower Park.

Why this hike matters

A long view across a compact Hill Country landscape.

Eisenhower Park protects 420 acres of wooded dry creek beds, rocky canyons, and upland habitat. Hillview follows the park’s outer terrain, connecting close observation of limestone and native plants with broad views from the ridge.

Habitat changes

Watch the vegetation shift between exposed limestone, Ashe juniper and live-oak woodland, dry drainage features, and sunnier grass-and-shrub openings.

Topographic story

The route’s bends and elevation changes reveal how thin soils, water movement, slope direction, and bedrock shape Hill Country plant communities.

Observation network

Trail markers identify plants along parts of Hillview. Community observations on eBird and iNaturalist add a living record of the park’s species.

Route notes

Follow the park’s outer green line.

Hillview Natural Trail

The current City trail map labels 1.72 miles of natural surface. The path traces much of the park perimeter through rolling, rocky terrain.

Hillview Paved Trail

A 0.30-mile paved segment near the parking and pavilion area completes the named Hillview connection shown on the current map.

Practical circuit

Combining the current map’s natural and paved Hillview segments yields approximately 2.02 miles. Exact GPS totals may vary at junctions.

Optional tower

The observation tower is reached by a separate 0.11-mile spur near the west side of the park. Add it only if time, heat, and footing allow.

Plan a responsible visit

  • Visit between sunrise and sunset.
  • Carry water and choose cooler morning hours in warm weather.
  • Wear shoes suited to uneven limestone and natural surfaces.
  • Keep leashed pets close and remove all waste.
  • Keep bicycles on the greenway; no bicycles, scooters, skateboards, or roller blades on park trails.
  • Stay on designated trails and leave plants, rocks, and wildlife undisturbed.

What to notice

Compare exposed ridge plants with shaded drainage vegetation, listen for woodland birds, and inspect—not collect—lichen, fossils, insects, and signs of mammals.
