Friedrich Wilderness Park Loop

A steep Hill Country circuit through oak-juniper woodland, rocky slopes, deep canyons, and protected bird habitat.

Why this hike matters

A wildlife sanctuary shaped by limestone, slope, and shade.

Friedrich Wilderness Park protects undeveloped Hill Country terrain and nearly ten miles of pedestrian trails. The City describes rare birds, terrestrial orchids, steep hills, and deep canyons within the preserve.

Habitat refuge

The park was donated with the instruction that native vegetation, trees, birds, and wildlife be protected and encouraged to use the land as a sanctuary.

Endangered songbirds

The City identifies nesting habitat for the golden-cheeked warbler and black-capped vireo. Remain on trail, keep voices low, and never pursue wildlife for a closer view.

Topographic variety

Main Loop moves between relatively level lower terrain and steep Level 4 ascents, reaching exposed upper slopes with views east and southeast.

Route notes

Main Loop, from the trailhead.

Entry Trail

The park’s only entrance is a 0.27-mile easy, paved woodland path. Walk it in both directions to reach and return from Main Loop.

Main Loop Trail

The 1.7-mile loop climbs approximately 350 feet. Lower portions are relatively level; the north and south hill ascents are steep.

Practical total

Main Loop plus the Entry Trail out and back is approximately 2.2 miles. GPS totals can vary with junction choices and wandering.

Trail conditions

Western trails have steep rocky sections that can be slippery when wet and extremely hot from May through October.

Plan a responsible visit

  • Visit between sunrise and sunset.
  • Bring plenty of drinking water; the remote trails have no water source.
  • Wear shoes suitable for steep, loose, and rocky surfaces.
  • Leave pets, bicycles, scooters, skateboards, alcohol, fires, and smoking at home.
  • Stay on trail and protect sensitive nesting habitat.
  • Check the City page for current alerts before leaving home.

What to notice

Compare shaded canyon and exposed ridge communities, look for seasonal orchids and native grasses, and listen before scanning the woodland canopy.
