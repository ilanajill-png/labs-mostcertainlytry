The measured grid

Three beds, one spiral, no thirsty chaos.

North is shown at the top and the entry at the south/front. The herb spiral begins two feet inside the front edge. If field north differs, rotate the whole plan while keeping the trellis and tallest crops on true north.

Every circle is one plant. Codes match the key below. The spiral is enlarged in the next section. Scroll sideways on a small screen. Final orientation still needs a field check for true north, direct-sun hours, and afternoon shade.

Bed A · large brassicas

Twenty-four-inch centers protect airflow and leave room for full heads and sprouts. Put fine insect mesh on immediately after planting.

Bed B · heads and stems

Cabbage and broccoli get the deeper rows; compact kohlrabi sits along the accessible southern edge for easy stem checks.

Bed C · repeat harvests

Leaf crops and quick roots use closer spacing. Harvest outer leaves, baby roots, and crowded starts early rather than letting the bed close over.

Companion-planting audit

Yes—but compatibility, not folklore.

The layout used shared moisture, mature size, shade, airflow, harvest access, and pest protection. It did not assume that one scented herb reliably protects another crop.

What works

Brassicas share irrigation and can sit beneath one insect-mesh system. Quick roots occupy accessible edges. Tall beans stay north so they do not shade the beds. Dry woody herbs are separated from thirsty greens.

What needs management

Grouping brassicas also concentrates brassica pests and disease. Fine mesh, weekly leaf checks, sanitation, and next-season rotation matter more than aromatic neighbors.

What is isolated

Fennel is containerized for size and root competition; aggressive lemon balm is contained; rue stays away from paths; rosemary and lemongrass remain outside the spiral because their mature dimensions are incompatible.

Hand watering

Check the soil before lifting the can.

Forecast rain is not irrigation until it falls. The morning decision should combine measured overnight rain, forecast temperature, wind, plant establishment, container exposure, and a two-inch finger test.

Zone 1

Large brassicas

Beds A and B need deep, even watering at individual root zones. New transplants are checked daily for 10–14 days; established plants are watered only when the upper root zone begins to dry.

Zone 2

Leaf crops and roots

Bed C has shallower roots and needs more frequent checks. Keep newly planted root crops gently moist without pounding or displacing their soil.

Zone 3

Spiral crown

Sage and marjoram must drain and partly dry between waterings. Do not give the whole spiral the same amount.

Zone 4

Spiral middle and base

Basil, Vietnamese cilantro, lemon balm, woodruff, and strawberries prefer evenly moist soil but not saturation.

Zone 5

Containers

Check every pot individually. Rosemary and rue dry between waterings; warm-weather lemongrass and Malabar spinach use considerably more.

Rain credit

One inch is a weekly benchmark

One inch over a 4 × 10-foot bed is about 25 gallons. It is a reference—not an instruction to pour 25 gallons at once. Soil, heat, wind, mulch, and actual rain determine the daily portion.

The morning sequence

1. Record overnight rainfall. 2. Check today’s high, wind, and rain probability. 3. Test soil two inches down. 4. Check containers and new transplants first. 5. Water slowly at soil level. 6. Skip anything still cool and moist.

Harvest forecasting

Dates begin the estimate. Plants finish it.

Days-to-maturity numbers are useful only when paired with planting dates, whether the crop began as seed or transplant, cultivar labels, weather setbacks, and physical readiness.

Record at planting

  • Date planted outdoors
  • Seed or nursery transplant
  • Tag photos and stated maturity
  • Pot/cell size and plant height
  • Desired baby, full-size, or repeat harvest

Watch during growth

  • First heading or button formation
  • Root or kohlrabi-stem swelling
  • First flower and pod set
  • Frost, heat, pest, or transplant shock
  • Weekly overview photographs

Harvest by readiness

  • Firm heads before loosening or bolting
  • Kohlrabi before stems turn woody
  • Brussels sprouts firm at 1–1.5 inches
  • Beans while pods remain tender
  • Herbs before heavy flowering for leaf flavor
