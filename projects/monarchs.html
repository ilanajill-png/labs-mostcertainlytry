What is riding on the butterfly?

CTT · BlūMorpho / Blū+

A solar-powered radio transmitter, reported at approximately 60 mg, developed by Cellular Tracking Technologies. Project Monarch uses compatible receivers and connected devices to detect tagged butterflies.

Technology and collaboration →

Lotek · NanoPin

A battery-powered VHF radio tag, reported at approximately 130 mg, used in other monarch studies through receiver networks such as Motus. This is not the transmitter used for the BlūMorpho collaboration below.

NH Audubon field research →

Monarch Watch · wing tags

Numbered adhesive labels, not electronic trackers. A location is recorded when someone finds the butterfly and reports its tag. Its tagging app is separate from Project Monarch Science.

Traditional tagging →

These transmitters are not miniature GPS trackers providing uninterrupted location histories. Detection coverage, sunlight or battery life, and the tracking system affect what can be observed.

How to interpret the annual records
  • 2024: Lionel’s November release at Cape May Point is individually documented. It appears under both CTT and the center, but is the same butterfly. No full annual total was published in the reviewed sources.
  • 2025: The contemporary announcement reports more than 400 deployed. A later feed's 525-tag consortium entry is not an organization-level allocation and is not treated here as a confirmed deployment total.
  • 2026: Records retain fully deployed, partially deployed, tags in hand, funded/expected and unspecified status. Combined targets can include multiple sites and seasons.
  • Unconfirmed attribution: The possible Fleming College match is not established by the feed. It must not be counted as a confirmed organization-specific record.
