Schertz Pollinator Garden

A compact demonstration garden showing how regionally appropriate plants become food, shelter, and stepping-stone habitat for butterflies, bees, and other insects.

Why this place matters

Small garden, working habitat.

The Guadalupe Chapter of the Native Plant Society of Texas maintains this public demonstration garden near the Schertz Community Garden. It turns native-plant advocacy into something visitors can inspect at human scale.

Plant relationships

Look for host plants that feed caterpillars, nectar plants that sustain adults, and varied bloom forms serving different pollinators.

Seasonal evidence

Flowers, seeds, leaf damage, dormant stems, and visiting insects reveal that habitat changes through the year.

Transferable ideas

Notice plant spacing, mulch, paths, water needs, and how a civic edge can become useful urban habitat.

Field notes

Read it as a network.

Flower → visitor

Watch which insects choose tubular, open, clustered, or composite flowers.

Leaf → life cycle

Chewed leaves are often evidence of successful host plants, not failed gardening.

Shade → survival

Compare exposed and protected areas during South Texas heat.

Garden → corridor

Consider how nearby yards, trees, and open spaces extend or interrupt habitat.

A calm visit plan

  • Visit in daylight and respect county and community-garden activity.
  • Stay on paths and do not collect plants, seeds, or insects.
  • Bring a close-focus camera or binoculars instead of handling wildlife.
  • Check the official chapter page for current plant charts and updates.
