Urban park field guide · New Braunfels

Fischer Park

New Braunfels' largest park: a 62-acre hilltop landscape where two ponds, greenway trails, a butterfly garden, and nature education meet everyday recreation.

Why this place matters

Nature beside ordinary life.

Fischer Park combines recreation with accessible nature learning. Its ponds, greenway trails, Nature Education Center, and butterfly garden make ecological observation possible without requiring a remote destination.

Pond edges

Reeds, open water, muddy margins, insects, fish, and visiting birds create changing zones to observe from the trail.

Pollinator garden

Flowers and host plants offer a close-range view of butterfly behavior and the plant choices that support it.

Hilltop perspective

The park's elevation and broad views invite comparisons between built New Braunfels and the surrounding Hill Country landscape.

Field notes

Walk from water to upland.

Waterline

Watch for birds, dragonflies, ripples, tracks, and changes in pond level.

Bloom

Compare which flowers attract butterflies, bees, and other insects.

Trail

Notice how shade, slope, wind, and nearby activity change what appears.

Education

Use current nature programs to add guided interpretation to a self-led visit.

A calm visit plan

  • Start early in warm weather and bring water and sun protection.
  • Share multi-use trails with walkers, runners, and cyclists.
  • Observe pond wildlife without feeding, handling, or crowding it.
  • Check City information for nature programs and current facility hours.
