The protected landscape

Honey from hives stewarded at Bracken Cave Preserve.

Bat Conservation International describes Bracken Cave Preserve as more than 1,500 acres of protected Texas habitat. It is the summer home of more than 15 million Mexican free-tailed bats and supports more than 1,700 additional documented species.

The harvest statement and feeding practice are supplied by TBD Honey. Preserve facts are sourced to Bat Conservation International. This page does not imply that BCI endorses or sponsors the product.

The harvest ledger

Every jar should answer the useful questions.

The bees are never given sugar water, and nothing is added to the harvested honey. Each limited batch will be bottled separately and identified by harvest date and batch number.

  1. 01

    Where were the hives?

    Record the preserve source while acknowledging that honey bees may forage beyond property boundaries.

  2. 02

    How were the bees fed?

    State the producer practice plainly: these bees are never given sugar water.

  3. 03

    What happened after harvest?

    Publish extraction date, heating and filtration statement, ingredients, net weight, and batch code.

  4. 04

    What does this batch taste like?

    Write notes from the jar in hand—color, aroma, texture, crystallization, bloom, and weather.

Claim discipline

No USDA Organic claim without certification; no pesticide-free claim without testing; no preserve-exclusive forage claim; no BCI logo or endorsement language without written permission.

Our approach

Measure what matters. Claim only what can be shown.

01

Bee-first evidence

Disclose the no-sugar-water practice and preserve source without stretching either claim beyond the records.

02

Ingredient clarity

The ingredient is honey. Nothing is added to the harvested batch.

03

Useful packaging

Clear glass, readable labels, lot coding, tamper evidence, and a stick carton carrying the full mandatory copy.

04

No medical theater

Honey is food. The preserve story describes provenance and habitat—not disease treatment or prevention.

FAQ

Before ordering opens.

Can I buy this harvest now?

Not yet. Winter 2026 ordering is planned, subject to final source records, labeling, food-law pathway, costs, and inventory.

Are the bees fed sugar water?

No. TBD Honey states that the bees producing this harvest are never given sugar water.

Is the honey organic or pesticide-free?

No certification or pesticide-free claim is being made here. Preserve sourcing does not by itself establish either claim, and honey bees can forage beyond property boundaries.

Is this an official Bat Conservation International product?

No endorsement or sponsorship is implied. Preserve facts link to BCI’s public information; use of BCI’s logo or partnership language would require written permission.

Why might honey crystallize?

Crystallization is a normal physical change influenced by floral composition, temperature, and time.

Is honey safe for infants?

No. Honey should not be fed to children under 12 months.
