Hiking field guide · South San Antonio

Medina River Natural Area Loop

Follow a short paved approach to the Rio Medina Trail, then circle through pecan and bald-cypress riparian forest beside one of San Antonio's defining waterways.

Why this hike matters

San Antonio's southern riparian natural area.

The 511-acre preserve is the city's only natural-area park on the south side and the only riparian site in its natural-areas system. The loop brings river forest, upland vegetation, historic-route interpretation, and community recreation into one compact outing.

River forest

Mature pecans and bald cypress mark the wetter corridor, while cactus and honey mesquite become more visible on higher, drier ground.

Habitat transition

Watch how soil moisture, shade, leaf litter, and plant density change as the route moves between pavilion grounds, river edge, and upland trail.

Water and people

The natural area also supports fishing access, a kayaking trail, camping, and a paved greenway. Notice how recreation is concentrated to protect the larger landscape.

Route notes

Use El Camino to reach the loop.

El Camino Trail

Begin near the pavilion on the 0.23-mile paved, Level 1 El Camino Trail. It reaches the river and connects with Rio Medina Trail.

Rio Medina Trail

Complete the 1.47-mile natural-surface loop through riparian forest, then return on El Camino. The combined trailhead outing is about 1.93 miles.

Level 3 access

Rio Medina can include steep slopes and is not classified as accessible. Expect roots, loose soil, uneven surfaces, and slick footing after rain.

Optional extension

The paved El Chaparral Greenway runs through the preserve and beyond it. Treat any extension as a separate out-and-back and track the added distance.

Plan a responsible visit

  • Visit between sunrise and sunset and check current park closures before leaving.
  • Carry water; the official map warns that water is not available on the trails.
  • Wear shoes suited to roots, uneven natural surface, and steep sections.
  • Keep pets leashed and remove all waste.
  • Swimming is prohibited; fishing requires a valid Texas fishing license.
  • Alcohol is prohibited. Camping is group-reservation only.

What to notice

Look for riparian birds, flowering plants and pollinators, floodplain tree structure, tracks near quiet edges, and seasonal changes in river flow and canopy.
