IoT White Paper · Las Jaras Freezer Safety · 2026-06-17

Turning a freezer scare into a household nervous-system sensor.

The GoveeLife Smart Thermometer R1 with Wi-Fi Gateway is being set up as a freezer monitor for Las Jaras. The point is practical: catch a warming freezer early enough to protect food, money, and household calm, without depending on Buddy to manually send screenshots.

Premise

The alert matters more than the chart.

A freezer sensor is useful only if it changes behavior before a loss happens. The Las Jaras setup treats the R1 as a small household safety service: a probe in the freezer, a gateway for remote monitoring, a Home Assistant or API path for machine-readable alerts, and a shared response script for what to do when the temperature rises.

GoveeLife's product page lists the R1 as Model H5108&B5108, with app alerts, remote monitoring through Govee Home, a probe range from -40°F to 158°F, anti-false-alarm delay, and two-year data storage. The FCC manual record identifies H5108 as Smart Thermometer R1.

Human Help Needed

  • Open the box, pull the battery tab, plug in the probe, and place the sensor.
  • Use the Govee Home phone app for pairing, Bluetooth permission, push notifications, and Wi-Fi credentials.
  • Enter Wi-Fi only inside the app. Do not send passwords, QR codes, serial numbers, or account screens.
  • Use limited setup screenshots only as a temporary fallback if pairing fails or the app shows an error.

What HOWDY Can Do

  • Identify the device, build the setup plan, and keep the public documentation privacy-safe.
  • Attempt Home Assistant, MQTT, local API, cloud API, or webhook visibility before approving the device.
  • Interpret the freezer trend against food-safety targets.
  • Create threshold, stale-data, unavailable, and battery alerts once entities are visible.
  • Update the Samsung freezer case with readings and next actions if requested.
  • Draft fallback response prompts for degraded-alert situations.

What The Human Owns

  • Physical placement: probe location, gasket check, cable routing, and battery access.
  • App control: account login, device pairing, Wi-Fi, phone permissions, and notification settings.
  • Food judgment: frozen solid, softening, leaking, thawed, or visibly unsafe.
  • Final decisions: whether to share alerts with another phone, return screenshot-only devices, or buy more sensors.

Approval Gate

No screenshot-only devices.

The freezer monitor is not approved for the Las Jaras house-alert stack until HOWDY can read it without Buddy manually sending screenshots. Acceptable paths include Home Assistant, MQTT, a local API, a cloud API, email/webhook forwarding, or another reliable automation route.

Minimum Signals

  • Current freezer temperature.
  • Last update timestamp or freshness signal.
  • Battery status if exposed.
  • Gateway/device online or unavailable state.
  • Alertable threshold crossings in Home Assistant or an equivalent alert bus.

Operating Rules

Green, yellow, orange, red.

Green

Freezer is at or below 0°F and food is frozen solid.

Yellow

Freezer is 1°F to 9°F or showing short spikes that recover quickly.

Orange

Freezer reaches 10°F or higher, repeats spikes, or recovers slowly.

Red

Food is softening, thawed, leaking, or freezer approaches or holds above 32°F.
