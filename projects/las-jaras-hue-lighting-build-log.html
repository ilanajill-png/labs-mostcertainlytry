Las Jaras Build Log · Hue Lighting Experiment

Can Hue and HOWDY fix our lighting without rewiring everything?

Las Jaras was built in 1952, which means charming old-house texture and lighting problems that feel very 1952: questionable switches, ceiling fans with pull strings instead of remotes, and a real dependence on floor and table lamps.

Premise

Can smart bulbs beat old-house lighting chaos?

Can we use Hue and our personal agent HOWDY PARTNER to fix the house without an electrician rewiring everything? The bet is not that smart bulbs solve every electrical problem. The bet is that a careful first phase can make daily lighting much better while we learn which fixtures actually need professional work.

HOWDY's role is to keep the project sequenced, name every device cleanly, build usable scenes, document skipped fixtures, and time the real install against the estimate.

First Pass

  • Install order: Living Room, Red Room, Blue Bathroom, Kitchen.
  • Do not touch: the 3 kitchen chandelier lights in this starting pass.
  • Sync path: Hue Sync TV app on Samsung at the planned $2.99/month subscription.
  • Reality check: start the timer and record actual duration for every task.

Prep Work Before Starting

The install was designed before the bulbs came out.

  • Mapped all lights in the house: room by room, including lamps, ceiling fans, fixture candidates, specialty-risk lights, and places where old switches or fixture uncertainty could make Hue a bad first move.
  • Created a mood/personality for each room: each room got a role, a Loteria/personality direction where available, and preselected Hue settings for arrival, daily use, task light, winding down, night paths, and room-specific rituals.

Why This Matters

We are not just replacing bulbs. We are testing whether an old house can feel more intentional by matching smart-light placement to how each room is supposed to behave.

The first install batch uses the already-mapped room order: Living Room, Red Room, Blue Bathroom, Kitchen, with the kitchen chandelier lights excluded until a separate fixture review.

Mood / Personality Lighting

Preselected Hue direction for the rooms we are installing

Living Room · El Sol

The warm center of the house: guest arrival, movie nights, conversation, dining, TV/audio, and character lighting around the Reese sectional and dining area.

  • Guest Arrival: warm welcome light that makes the room feel open, social, and flattering.
  • Movie Night: low warm lamps plus the Hue Play gradient as TV/media glow, with glare kept off the screen.
  • Conversation: soft amber and cream light for sitting, talking, and lingering without overhead harshness.
  • Dining / Lingering Dinner: warm table-adjacent glow, not a full-room blast.
  • Cleaning / Reset: bright warm white so the room can actually be cleaned and reset.

Red Room · El Corazon

Grounded, protective, sensual-but-guest-appropriate, hotel-inflected, richly textured, and collected.

  • Guest Arrival: rose gold, warm blush, candle amber, and a very soft root-red accent.
  • Ruby Grounding: deeper ruby and clay-amber tones for a rooted, calm guest room mood.
  • Rose Reading: soft rose and warm amber bright enough to read without turning the room white.
  • Ember Wind Down: ember red, smoked amber, dim rose, and near-candle orange.
  • Night Path: dim red or amber low enough for sleep but safe enough to move through the room.
  • Cleaning: practical warm white at high brightness.

Blue Bathroom · Cool Spa Reset

A cool, clean bathroom mood: blue-white clarity for getting ready, soft spa tones for decompression, and very low night light where the fixture is safe.

  • Blue Bathroom Bright: clean warm-white/soft daylight balance for getting ready without going harsh.
  • Blue Bathroom Spa: soft aqua, warm white, and a little muted blue for a calm reset.
  • Morning Rinse: pale sky blue, clean white, and a small warm note so it still feels human.
  • Getting Ready: flattering warm white with a tiny cool-blue edge for clarity.
  • Steam Down: dim aqua, muted blue, and warm cream for evening decompression.
  • Night Path: very low blue or warm-white, around 5-8% where safe.
  • Cleaning: warm white at full practical brightness.

Kitchen · El Cazo

The cobalt working room: coffee, prep, hosting, groceries, ordinary household reset, and bright practical light without turning the kitchen into chaos.

  • Kitchen Prep: bright warm task light with clean counters and enough visibility for cooking.
  • Warm Daily Use: warm white with soft lemon and cobalt-friendly accents for everyday movement.
  • Hosting Glow: candle amber and warm highlights for drinks, food, and people lingering nearby.
  • Kitchen Night: very low amber/warm light for passing through without waking up the whole house.
  • Cleaning / Reset: bright warm white for dishes, counters, pantry intake, and end-of-day reset.
  • First-pass rule: exclude the 3 chandelier lights until their fixture type, heat, and bulb compatibility are reviewed.

What Counts as Success

Better lighting before bigger electrical work.

Success means the house gets more usable lighting control, the TV gradient setup works, scenes are named and understandable, physical switches remain sane, and skipped fixtures are documented honestly.

What Still Needs a Human

Safety stays boring.

Any questionable switch, tight fixture, damp location, chandelier, dimmer conflict, or ladder-heavy surprise gets paused. Smart bulbs are allowed to be useful; they are not allowed to pretend bad wiring is fine.
