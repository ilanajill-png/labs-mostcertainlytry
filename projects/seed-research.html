Planting Logic

For Schertz/San Antonio heat, the practical rule outranks the moon rule: sow in the evening, hydrate the medium before planting, water gently after sowing, and protect seedlings from west-afternoon heat until they are established.

  • Cover seeds roughly two to three times as deep as the seed is wide.
  • Keep the surface damp enough to prevent crusting, but not saturated.
  • Use shade cloth, porch shade, or filtered light for tender starts during hot weeks.
  • Track germination by seed, packet source, and method so weak packets can be retested or discarded.

Moon Note

The original planning note treated June 29, 2026 as a full moon seed day. Catalina Sky Survey marks June 29, 2026 as Full Moon. Royal Museums Greenwich lists the precise full moon at 12:57am on June 30 in UK time, which corresponds to the evening of June 29 in Central Time.

Traditional lunar gardening would frame the full moon as a peak-moisture moment for above-ground crops, then move into waning-moon work for roots, perennials, pruning, sorting, and soil work. Treat that as folklore and ritual timing; temperature, moisture, seed age, and medium are the actual success levers.

Good Candidates

  • Heat-tolerant vegetables and flowers: okra, southern peas/cowpeas, cucumbers, zucchini, zinnias, marigolds, and other warm-season annuals.
  • Fall-prep starts in protected trays: peppers, tomatoes, onions, celery, and herbs where heat protection is available.
  • Native or wildflower packets marked for fall: hold for August-November direct sowing unless running a controlled germination test.

Skip Or Delay

  • Cool-season crops outdoors in summer heat: lettuce, cilantro, carrots, radishes, brassicas, and beets are better held for fall windows.
  • Direct sowing without daily moisture checks; hot soil can fail a packet fast.
  • Unlabeled seed starts. Label seed, variety, source, method, and start date before water touches the tray.
