Planting Logic

For Schertz/San Antonio heat, the practical rule outranks the moon rule: sow in the evening, hydrate the medium before planting, water gently after sowing, and protect seedlings from west-afternoon heat until they are established.

  • Cover seeds roughly two to three times as deep as the seed is wide.
  • Keep the surface damp enough to prevent crusting, but not saturated.
  • Use shade cloth, porch shade, or filtered light for tender starts during hot weeks.
  • Track germination by seed, packet source, and method so weak packets can be retested or discarded.

Moon Note

The original planning note treated June 29, 2026 as a full moon seed day. Catalina Sky Survey marks June 29, 2026 as Full Moon. Royal Museums Greenwich lists the precise full moon at 12:57am on June 30 in UK time, which corresponds to the evening of June 29 in Central Time.

Traditional lunar gardening would frame the full moon as a peak-moisture moment for above-ground crops, then move into waning-moon work for roots, perennials, pruning, sorting, and soil work. Treat that as folklore and ritual timing; temperature, moisture, seed age, and medium are the actual success levers.

Good Candidates

  • Heat-tolerant vegetables and flowers: okra, southern peas/cowpeas, cucumbers, zucchini, zinnias, marigolds, and other warm-season annuals.
  • Fall-prep starts in protected trays: peppers, tomatoes, onions, celery, and herbs where heat protection is available.
  • Native or wildflower packets marked for fall: hold for August-November direct sowing unless running a controlled germination test.

Skip Or Delay

  • Cool-season crops outdoors in summer heat: lettuce, cilantro, carrots, radishes, brassicas, and beets are better held for fall windows.
  • Direct sowing without daily moisture checks; hot soil can fail a packet fast.
  • Unlabeled seed starts. Label seed, variety, source, method, and start date before water touches the tray.

Seed Snail Origins

The clearest origin trail points to Farida Sober, a UK home gardener in South Wales, as the person who developed and popularized the modern seed-snail method. Public profiles and interviews describe the method as something she built out of necessity: limited space, ordinary materials, and a need to start many seedlings without constant potting-up.

The public record is a little messy because social posts are duplicated, reshared, and often hidden behind login walls. I did not find a single universally verifiable "first" post, but the strongest early/original trail is Farida's own Instagram account, her seed-snail reels, and later gardening articles that credit her as originator or popularizer.

Method Story

  • Kotona Living's interview says Farida had been using seed rolls for about four years, starting with onions and leeks, then refining the system into upright rolls of thin compost on plastic/foam wrap.
  • Better Homes & Gardens describes Farida as the South Wales gardener who developed and popularized the Seed Snail Method to grow more seedlings in less space.
  • Hartley Botanic describes "seed snails" as a term popularized by Farida Sober on Instagram, with rolls stored upright and unrolled for transplanting or adding more mix.
  • Humans Who Grow Food shared Farida's story and credits her with a method she developed and shared that went viral among gardeners.

Original / Early Post Trail

  • Farida Sober on Instagram, @faridasober: primary account and best current starting point; public snippets mention the Seed Snail journey and her Seed Snail e-book.
  • Instagram reel C5n_BlpoflI: repeatedly cited in Facebook gardening-group posts as an early Farida seed-snail reel people discovered and copied.
  • Instagram reel DFk42zhsCCt: public search-visible seed-snail reel tied to the Farida Sober discussion trail.
  • Humans Who Grow Food profile/post: origin-story reference and social-link roundup for Farida.

Accounts Found

  • Instagram: @faridasober verified by public Instagram/search snippets.
  • Facebook: facebook.com/s.farida.sober, referenced by Humans Who Grow Food and reachable, though Facebook limits logged-out inspection.
  • YouTube: Farida Sober channel; third-party pages also refer to her YouTube handle as @Adirafs.
  • TikTok: no official Farida Sober account was verified from public search. TikTok and reposted short-video content frequently credit @faridasober, but I would not treat any TikTok profile as hers unless she links it from Instagram/Facebook.
  • E-book / guide: Farida's Instagram public snippets mention a Seed Snail e-book linked from her bio; I did not find a stable direct public URL outside the social bio trail.
