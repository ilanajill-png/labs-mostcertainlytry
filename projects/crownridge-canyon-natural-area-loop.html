Why this hike matters

A protected aquifer landscape inside the city.

Crownridge Canyon was the first City natural area developed through San Antonio’s Edwards Aquifer Protection ballot initiative. Its 210.5 acres protect a sequence of restored grassland, limestone slopes, wooded canyon bottoms, and aquifer-recharge terrain.

Two trail experiences

The stabilized Red Oak Trail is a Level 1 route designed to meet ADA accessibility requirements. Bear Grass is a Level 4 natural-surface loop with rocky ledges and a few steeper slopes.

Canyon to canopy

The preserve’s signature sequence includes forested canyon bottoms, hillside vistas, restored grasslands, and a canopy-level bridge overlook.

Rare-bird habitat

Friends of San Antonio Natural Areas identifies the preserve as habitat for the endangered golden-cheeked warbler during nesting season. Observe quietly and remain on trail.

Route notes

Choose one loop—or link both.

Red Oak Trail

A 1.3-mile lower loop on stabilized base, rated Level 1 and designed to meet ADA accessibility requirements. It crosses Red Oak Canyon at the bridge.

Bear Grass Trail

A 0.6-mile upper loop on Level 4 natural surface. The official description rates it medium difficulty, with rocky ledges and a few steep slopes.

Linked outing

Walking both named loops produces an approximately 1.9-mile outing. Connector use, backtracking, and GPS recording can change the actual total.

Wayfinding

Carry the official map and follow posted trail signs. Do not interpret the linked mileage as a separately named City trail.

Plan a responsible visit

  • Visit between 7:30 a.m. and sunset.
  • Leave dogs and bicycles at home; both are prohibited.
  • Carry water and sun protection; exposed grassland can heat quickly.
  • Use Red Oak for the gentler, accessible option.
  • Stay on trail, especially during golden-cheeked warbler nesting season.
  • Check current conditions and rules before leaving home.

What to notice

Compare restored grassland with shaded canyon, watch for changes in limestone exposure and plant communities, and listen for birds before searching the canopy.
