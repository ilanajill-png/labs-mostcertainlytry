Hiking field guide · San Antonio

Comanche Lookout Loop

A short linked loop through wooded slopes to the stone tower and one of Bexar County’s highest points.

Why this hike matters

A high point where ecology and city history meet.

Comanche Lookout Park protects 96 acres on an escarpment between the Gulf Coastal Plain and Edwards Plateau, rising to 1,340 feet—the fourth-highest point in Bexar County.

Wooded hill

City records describe ash juniper, Texas and Mexican buckeye, Lindheimer hackberry, honey mesquite, huisache, and other native and naturalized vegetation on the slope.

Stone landmark

The unfinished medieval-style tower was begun in the 1920s by retired Army Colonel Edward H. Coppock and remains the park’s most visible cultural feature.

Old travel corridor

Nacogdoches Road follows the El Camino Real de los Tejas corridor along the hill’s base, connecting this urban park to a much longer human landscape.

Route notes

Short mileage, steady climbing.

Route type

A 1.1-mile linked loop through the park. It is not identical to the City map’s separately named 0.8-mile Comanche Loop.

Climbing

The community route reports 138 feet of elevation gain and a moderate difficulty rating.

Wayfinding

The official map labels Comanche, Tower, Deer, and Library loops. Carry it and follow posted junction signs.

Trail surfaces

Mapped routes use concrete, aggregate, and gravel. Consult the City accessibility map for grade, cross-slope, width, and surface details.

Plan a responsible visit

  • Visit between sunrise and sunset.
  • Use the official park map and confirm your chosen linked loop at each junction.
  • Carry water and sun protection, even for a short route.
  • Stay on established trails and leave the tower and remnant foundations undisturbed.
  • Check current City rules and conditions before leaving home.

What to notice

Compare sun-exposed slopes with shaded woodland, listening for birds and looking for seasonal flowers, fruit, insects, and changes in canopy cover.
