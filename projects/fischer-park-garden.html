How to Visit

Come for a close look at a working pollinator habitat. The City describes the garden as a place for demonstration, classes, community involvement and guided or self-guided walks.

Find the garden

Fischer Park
1935 Hilltop Summit Road
New Braunfels, TX 78130

Use park signage to find the Nature Education Center and butterfly garden. Check the City’s current information for access, programs and facility hours.

  • Slow downObserve from the paths. Look for feeding, egg-laying, caterpillar activity and changing blooms without handling wildlife.
  • Look beyond monarchsThe City also highlights Queens, Gulf Fritillaries and other butterflies. A waystation supports a wider community.
  • Bring a field notebookRecord the date, survey-area number and photographs. A leaf, flower and whole-plant view will help with identification later.
  • Leave the garden growingKeep out of planted beds and leave flowers, seeds and insects in place. Use the benches to sit and watch.

More than a flower stop.

A useful butterfly garden supports different needs at different life stages.

01 / Feed

Nectar for adults

Flowers provide fuel for adult butterflies. Observe which blooms are being visited and how that changes through the seasons.

02 / Grow

Host plants for young

Monarch caterpillars depend on milkweed. Other butterfly species use different hosts. Nectar visits alone do not establish a plant as a caterpillar host.

03 / Return

Habitat across seasons

The City describes native berry-producing plants, larval plants and bird resources here. Our survey will document the individual plants and where they grow.
