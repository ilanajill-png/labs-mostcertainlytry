Why this place matters

The clearest mission landscape for seeing food, irrigation, trade, and river ecology together.

Mission San Juan’s agricultural story extends far beyond its compact church compound. Irrigated fields, orchards, workshops, ranching, and trade made the mission a regional supplier. Today the demonstration farm and Yanaguana Trail help reconnect the architecture to water, food, wildlife, and working land.

The farm is part of the mission

The San Antonio Food Bank partnership uses the historic acequia to support a living demonstration farm southwest of the compound.

Yanaguana Trail

The paved, accessible river trail follows a stretch of the original San Antonio River where visitors may encounter turtles, owls, snakes, and other wildlife.

A regional supplier

Mission residents cultivated corn, beans, squash, peppers, melons, peaches, sweet potatoes, and sugar cane while ranches supplied livestock and trade goods.

Address

9101 Graf Road · San Antonio

World Heritage context

This is one of five components in the UNESCO San Antonio Missions World Heritage Site. It remains a distinct place with its own operator, access rules, and living history.

Plan a responsible visit

  • Walk the mission compound, then follow the signed route southwest toward the demonstration farm.
  • Use the paved Yanaguana Trail for a quieter river-focused extension; stay alert for wildlife and changing water conditions.
  • Park facilities generally operate 9 a.m.–5 p.m.; exterior grounds are open sunrise–sunset.
  • Admission and parking are free. The active parish church can close for services.
  • Pets are welcome on grounds and trails on a six-foot-or-shorter leash, but not inside the church or federal buildings.

Observe without erasing history

Nature and culture are intertwined here. Notice water, stone, plants, animals, heat, and shade while respecting active worship, descendant communities, archaeology, and protected structures.
