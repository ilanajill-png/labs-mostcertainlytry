Hiking field guide · San Antonio

McAllister Park Loop

The Blue Loop traces a long urban-wildland circuit through live-oak and juniper woodland, grass openings, pale limestone soil, and the upper Salado Creek watershed.

Why this hike matters

A substantial nature circuit inside the city.

McAllister Park protects 976 acres on San Antonio's north side. Its long natural-surface trail network offers a useful view of how woodland, grassland, drainage, recreation, and flood-control infrastructure coexist in a heavily developed urban landscape.

Urban refuge

Despite surrounding development, the park still supports wildlife and a broad mix of habitats. Listen and look most carefully where woodland edges meet clearings and drainage features.

Long-loop rhythm

The Blue Loop is long enough for habitat changes to become apparent. Notice differences in shade, soil depth, exposed limestone, plant density, and human activity around the perimeter.

Shared trail

McAllister's natural trails are used by hikers, runners, and mountain bikers. Stay alert at curves and intersections, keep right when practical, and yield predictably.

Route notes

Follow blue—and keep the map close.

Blue Loop

The official City map marks the long perimeter route in blue. The commonly recorded hiking route is 6.2 miles; GPS totals can vary with parking, connectors, and missed turns.

Navigation

The network has many crossings and optional spurs. Download or photograph the official map before starting and confirm each junction rather than relying on color alone.

Surface

Expect natural trail with limestone, roots, loose rock, exposed sections, and occasional mud after rain. The modest total climb is spread across rolling terrain.

Shorter alternatives

The official map also shows shorter paved and natural routes. Use those when heat, daylight, trail traffic, or footing makes the full Blue Loop a poor choice.

Plan a responsible visit

  • Park hours are 5 a.m.–11 p.m.; confirm current notices before leaving.
  • Carry enough water for a six-mile outing and favor cooler morning hours.
  • Wear shoes suited to uneven limestone and natural surfaces.
  • Watch for runners and bicycles, especially at blind turns and junctions.
  • Keep dogs leashed outside the designated dog park and remove all waste.
  • Stay on mapped trails and leave plants, rocks, and wildlife undisturbed.

What to notice

Compare woodland and grass-edge birds, flowering plants and pollinators, limestone-loving species, tracks near quiet trail edges, and seasonal changes around drainage areas.
