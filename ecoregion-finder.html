    Privacy

    This page does not use a database, does not write to browser storage, and does not intentionally log submitted addresses.

    What leaves the page
    The address is sent to Esri's public geocoder to convert it into a coordinate. The map also requests basemap and label tiles from Esri.
    What the ecoregion service receives
    The matched coordinate and visible map bounds are sent to the EPA ecoregion service. When optional layers are enabled, the visible bounds are also requested from USGS, USDA NRCS, and ArcGIS-hosted Texas geology or TWDB-derived aquifer services. The regional Carrizo–Wilcox layer is a static Labs-hosted compilation and sends no additional coordinate request.
    What Labs saves
    Nothing from your submission. Results are shown in your browser and disappear when you leave or refresh the page. Third-party map services may retain standard request logs under their own policies.
    Addresses are sensitive. Use a nearby intersection or landmark if exact-house precision is not needed.

    Levels

    EPA ecoregions are nested from broad continental patterns to finer local ecological areas.

    Broadest Level I

    Continental Region

    The largest ecological zones, shaped by broad patterns of climate, landforms, vegetation, and ecosystems.

    Broad Level II

    Subregion

    Subdivisions of Level I that narrow the map by more specific climate, geology, terrain, and vegetation patterns.

    Regional Level III

    Regional Ecoregion

    The scale that is often easiest to recognize by name, describing a meaningful regional ecological landscape.

    Most Detailed Level IV

    Local Ecoregion

    The finest mapped subdivision, reflecting more local differences in soils, terrain, vegetation, and land use.
